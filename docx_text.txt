1. Problem and vision
ChargeShare turns idle private EV chargers into an emergency rescue network, so a driver with 5% battery can find, book and reach a nearby owner's charger in minutes instead of waiting for a tow.
Problem (PS-5): EV drivers get stranded when the battery is low and public chargers are out of reach, occupied or broken. Meanwhile many EV owners have compatible private chargers (home, society parking, office) that sit unused most of the day and that they would share, but there is no convenient, trusted way to connect the two sides.
Vision: "Never stranded. Every charger counts." A PWA that works from a single SOS tap, with no app-store install.
Users
Stranded driver (requester): low battery, stressed, needs a fast answer. Cares about reachability, connector match, price, safety.
Charger host: EV owner or society resident with an idle charger. Cares about earning, privacy, control over when and who.
Secondary users (stretch story): cab and delivery fleet drivers on e-2W/e-3W/e-4W who lose earning time when they cannot charge.
Working name options: ChargeShare (given in the problem statement), ChargeBuddy, VoltRescue. Keep ChargeShare for the demo.
2. Competitor analysis
No mainstream app combines emergency-first matching, private chargers and a trust layer; most players only list fixed public stations. This table is from memory (approximate), so verify and update it with Perplexity prompt R1 in section 9 before presenting.
Gaps we fill
Private chargers are invisible to existing apps, so supply is untapped.
Nobody ranks options by whether the driver can actually reach them with the battery left.
No one offers a trust loop (verified hosts, ratings, price caps, safe handover) for strangers sharing power.
Zero new hardware: supply grows as fast as owners sign up.

4. Workflow

rescue flow Â· 7 steps, 1 decision, 1 loop
The driver flow runs left to right across the top, drops to the host for the accept decision, and a decline loops back to request the next host.
Host flow: sign up as Host, add a charger (connector, kW, price with AI suggestion, hours, map pin, rules), toggle Available, receive a request with the driver's battery and ETA, Accept or Decline within 2 minutes, confirm arrival (QR or button), start and stop the session, receive payout and a review.
Edge cases to handle in the UI
No reachable host: show the nearest public charger and a "call roadside help" button instead of an empty screen.
Host does not answer in 2 minutes: auto-expire the request and suggest the next ranked host.
Driver cancels: free before acceptance, small fee after the host is on the way.
Wrong connector: block the Request button with the reason shown on the card.

5. USP
Positioning line: "Other apps help you find a charger. ChargeShare rescues you when you are about to run out."
Emergency-first design. The home screen is one big SOS button, not a search bar. Input is battery % and connector, nothing else.
Range-aware matching. Only hosts the driver can actually reach with the battery left are shown as "Reachable"; the rest are greyed out. Competitors list stations without checking this.
Untapped supply, zero infrastructure. Uses chargers that already exist and sit idle, so the network grows without capital cost.
Trust layer for strangers. Verified hosts, ratings, price caps, house rules, QR check-in and an SOS share link.
Win-win economics. Driver pays a fair per-kWh price with a capped rescue fee; host earns from an asset that earned nothing before.
PWA reach. Opens from a WhatsApp link, installs to the home screen, no app-store step during an emergency.
Impact angle for judges: fewer tow-truck calls, less range anxiety (the main barrier to EV adoption), and better use of existing power.
6. AI features
Five AI features, each built so the app still works if the AI call fails (deterministic fallback), because demo Wi-Fi is unreliable. Runtime prompts for each are in section 10.
Why this is credible: A2 is deterministic and explainable (judges can see the formula), while A1, A3, A4 and A5 use the LLM only where language understanding adds value.
Stretch AI: demand prediction for hosts ("Evenings near the station are busy; open your charger 6 to 9 pm"), and a multilingual voice SOS in Hindi and Marathi.
Integration rule: never put the API key in the client. Call the LLM from a Supabase Edge Function or a Vercel serverless function. Gemini Flash or Groq free tiers are enough.
7. PWA tech stack, architecture and data model
Use React + Vite + Tailwind with Supabase, because it gives auth, database, realtime and storage with almost no backend code, and it fits the Lovable or Bolt workflow you used before.
Architecture in one line: PWA client talks to Supabase (auth, rows, realtime subscriptions) and to one Edge Function (/ai) that calls the LLM; the Rescue Score runs in the client from host rows.
Data model
requests.status values: requested, accepted, on_the_way, charging, completed, declined, cancelled.
Seed data: insert 15 to 20 fake hosts around your demo city so the map is never empty on stage (use Perplexity prompt R4 and R8 for realistic values).

Runtime prompt A3: explain charge (system)
You are a calm EV rescue assistant. Given JSON with battery_pct, kwh_needed, charger_kw, price_per_kwh, minutes_needed, km_gained, distance_to_host_km, write ONE reassuring sentence (max 30 words) telling the driver how long to charge and what that achieves, plus one short tip if distance to host is more than 70% of remaining range (suggest eco driving: no AC, keep under 40 km/h). Use only the numbers given.
Runtime prompt A4: screen listing or message (system)
You are a trust and safety reviewer for a peer-to-peer EV charging marketplace. Flag: requests to pay outside the app, sharing OTP or card details, unsafe or isolated meeting places, unrealistic prices, abusive language, identity or contact harvesting. Be conservative but do not block normal messages.
Runtime prompt A5: suggest price (system)

11. Demo script, pitch and risks
Three-minute demo script
Hook (20 s): "It is 9 pm, your EV shows 6%, the nearest public charger is dead. What now?"
SOS (30 s): On the phone, speak: "Nexon EV, 6 percent, near the station, need CCS2." The form fills itself (A1).
Results (40 s): Map shows hosts; two are greyed "Too far", three are "Reachable". Open "How we rank" to show the formula (A2).
Request (30 s): Tap Request; on the laptop, the host sees it and taps Accept; the phone updates live.
Charge and pay (30 s): Show the AI sentence (A3), the cost, mock UPI pay, then the review.
Host side and close (30 s): Earnings dashboard and the fair-price assistant (A5). Close: "Zero new hardware. Every idle charger becomes a rescue point."
Pitch structure: problem with one real story (R9), solution, live demo, how it works (range-aware matching, trust layer), market and revenue, roadmap.
Revenue model: a 10 to 15 percent service fee per session, host subscription for featured listing, and B2B plans for fleets and housing societies.
